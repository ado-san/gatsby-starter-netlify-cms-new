Somewhere in a drawer, you probably have an old laptop that still works but that nobody uses anymore. For a school in a remote area, that same laptop, cleaned and loaded with educational software, can become a library, a science lab and a programming class. That is exactly what **Labdoo** does.

## What is Labdoo?

[Labdoo](https://www.labdoo.org/) is a humanitarian social network created in 2010 and run by volunteers around the world. Its mission is to bring unused laptops, tablets and e-readers to schools in underserved regions, in developing countries but also in the developed world, **without economic or environmental cost**.

Three principles make it unique:

1. **No money changes hands.** Devices are donated, refurbished by volunteers and delivered for free.
2. **No extra CO₂ for transport.** Laptops travel in the luggage of people who are already going somewhere: tourists, business travellers, NGO staff.
3. **Full traceability.** Every device (a "dootronic") gets an ID and can be tracked on the platform, from donor to school, until it is recycled properly at the end of its life.

## The journey of a laptop

1. **Donation.** You bring your old laptop to a Labdoo "hub", a local volunteer point, or ship it to one.
2. **Sanitisation.** Volunteers erase all your personal data securely. This step is important: your data is wiped before the device goes anywhere.
3. **Installation.** The laptop is loaded with a free Linux system and a large set of offline educational content.
4. **Tagging.** The device is registered on the platform with its own page.
5. **Travel.** A traveller ("dootrip") who goes near a school in need carries one or two laptops in their luggage.
6. **Delivery and follow-up.** The school receives the laptops and reports back. Old devices are recycled responsibly at the end of their life.

## Free software at the heart of the project

Labdoo laptops run a Linux distribution (historically **Edubuntu**, the education flavour of Ubuntu, now a Labdoo-maintained Ubuntu-based image). Choosing free software is not a detail:

- **No licence costs.** Thousands of laptops can be equipped without paying for a single licence.
- **Old hardware, new life.** Lightweight Linux systems run well on computers that are ten years old.
- **Offline content.** Schools without a reliable connection get Wikipedia offline, Khan Academy videos, language courses, maths and science applications (GCompris, Scratch, Stellarium, LibreOffice, etc.).
- **Knowledge to share.** Students can study, modify and redistribute the software, which is the spirit of the four freedoms of free software.

## How you can help

- **Donate a device.** Laptops, tablets and e-readers in working condition, with their charger. Find the closest hub on the [Labdoo map](https://www.labdoo.org/).
- **Become a traveller.** Before your next holiday or business trip, check if there is a school near your destination and carry a laptop with you. It is a beautiful way to meet the local community.
- **Volunteer as a hub.** If you have some technical skills, you can sanitise and install laptops, or organise a collection in your company.
- **Talk about it.** Companies renew their IT equipment every three to five years; a single message to your IT department can turn a pallet of old laptops into a classroom.

The Labdoo website also has a great set of [videos](https://www.labdoo.org/content/labdoo-videos) explaining each step.

## Why it matters

Electronic waste is one of the fastest growing waste streams in the world, while millions of students still have no access to a computer. Labdoo connects both problems with an elegant, decentralised and almost zero-cost solution powered by volunteers and **open source software**. Extending the life of a laptop is also the most effective way to reduce its environmental footprint.
