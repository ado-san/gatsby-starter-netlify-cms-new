"Why would anyone invest in a company that gives its product away?" For a long time, this was the main objection to **open source startups**. Today, open source runs the internet, the cloud and AI, and **commercial open source software (COSS)** companies attract billions in **venture capital**. Here is how it works.

## Free software and open source: a quick reminder

- **Free software**, as defined by the Free Software Foundation, guarantees four freedoms: to run the program, to study and modify it, to redistribute copies, and to distribute modified versions.
- **Open source**, as defined by the Open Source Initiative, focuses on the same licences from a more pragmatic, development-oriented angle.

Licences fall into two families: **permissive** (MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD), which allow almost any reuse, and **copyleft** (GPL, AGPL), which require derived works to stay free. The choice of licence is a strategic business decision.

## Why open source wins

- **Distribution**: developers can try the product in minutes, without a sales call. Adoption is bottom-up.
- **Trust**: the code can be audited, which matters for security and **digital sovereignty**.
- **Community**: contributors improve the product, report bugs and create integrations.
- **Talent**: engineers want to work on public code that builds their reputation.
- **Standards**: open projects tend to become the default (Linux, Kubernetes, PostgreSQL, React).

## Business models that work

1. **Support and subscriptions.** The historic model of **Red Hat**, acquired by IBM in 2019 for about 34 billion dollars: the software is free, the enterprise subscription (support, certifications, long-term maintenance) is paid.
2. **Open core.** A free community edition and paid enterprise features (SSO, audit, advanced security). Used by GitLab, Mattermost and many others.
3. **Managed cloud / SaaS.** The company hosts its own software as a service: MongoDB Atlas, Elastic Cloud, Supabase, Vercel for Next.js. Customers pay for convenience and reliability.
4. **Dual licensing.** Free under a copyleft licence, paid licence for companies that want to embed it in proprietary products.
5. **Services and integration.** Consulting, training and custom development around an ecosystem: the model of many European integrators, and of **Nextcloud**, which sells enterprise subscriptions to governments and companies.
6. **Open models, paid platform.** In AI, **Mistral AI** publishes open-weight models while selling API access, enterprise deployments and its assistant, Le Chat.

## How venture capital looks at open source

VCs invest in COSS companies because open source adoption is a powerful **leading indicator**: GitHub stars, downloads, contributors and community activity show product-market fit before revenue. Specialised funds and indexes track the fastest-growing open source startups.

What investors look for:

- a **project with real adoption**, ideally led by its original creators;
- a clear **monetisation path** that does not conflict with the community;
- a **cloud or enterprise offer** with strong gross margins;
- a defensible position: brand, expertise, ecosystem, not just the code.

## The licence change lesson

Some companies, facing cloud providers reselling their software, moved from open source to "source-available" licences: **Elastic** (2021), **HashiCorp** with the Business Source License (2023), **Redis** (2024). The community answered with forks: **OpenSearch**, **OpenTofu**, **Valkey**, the latter two hosted by the Linux Foundation. Elastic later re-added an open source licence (AGPL) in 2024, and Redis did the same in 2025.

Lessons for founders:

- choose your licence **from day one** with your business model in mind (AGPL protects better against cloud resale than MIT);
- your community is your moat: breaking its trust can create a competitor overnight;
- governance matters: a foundation can reassure large contributors and customers.

## Open source, AI and Europe

AI has brought the open versus closed debate back to the centre. Open-weight models allow companies to run AI on sovereign infrastructure, fine-tune it on their data and avoid dependency on a single provider. For Europe, which has few hyperscalers, **open source is a lever for technological sovereignty and for building global ventures**, from Mistral AI to Nextcloud, and from Hugging Face (founded by French entrepreneurs) to countless startups.

## Advice for founders

- **Start with a real problem** developers have, and solve it in the open.
- **Measure community metrics** but focus on **active users** in companies, the future customers.
- **Make the paid offer obvious**: hosting, security, compliance, support.
- **Raise when adoption is proven**: open source traction is a strong signal for seed and Series A investors.
- **Stay humble with the community**: transparency about the roadmap and the business model builds lasting trust.
