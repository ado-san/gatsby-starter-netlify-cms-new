When we talk about crypto-currencies, two images come to mind: speculation, and gigantic mining farms consuming as much electricity as entire countries. **Ğ1** (pronounced "June") takes a completely different path. It uses a blockchain, but without energy-hungry mining, and its main innovation is not technical: it is a new way of **creating money**.

## The Relative Theory of Money

Ğ1 is the first currency built on the **Relative Theory of Money** (TRM, *Théorie Relative de la Monnaie*), published in 2010 by the French engineer Stéphane Laborde. The theory is available for free, in English, on [trm.creationmonetaire.info](https://en.trm.creationmonetaire.info/).

Its starting question is simple: *who should create money, and how?*

- In our current system, money is mostly created by commercial banks when they issue loans, and by central banks.
- In Bitcoin, money is created by miners as a reward for securing the network, which favours the early adopters and those who own the most computing power.

The TRM proposes a third way, based on four economic freedoms, including the freedom to produce value and to exchange in the currency of your choice, and on one principle: **symmetry in time and space**. Every human being, today and in the future, should have the same share in the creation of money.

## The Universal Dividend

In practice, each member of the Ğ1 currency receives every day a **Universal Dividend (UD)**: a fixed share of new money. The amount is recalculated periodically so that the money supply grows by about **10% per year**, a rate derived from human life expectancy in the theory.

Consequences:

- Money is not created by a bank or a company, but **co-created equally by all members**.
- A newcomer who joins in ten years receives the same relative share as someone who joined at the launch. There is no "early adopter jackpot".
- Prices are best measured *in UD*, a unit that stays stable relative to the total money supply.

## A Web of Trust instead of mining

To prevent anyone from creating fake accounts and receiving multiple dividends, Ğ1 uses a **Web of Trust**:

- to become a member, you must be certified by **at least five existing members** who know you personally;
- certifications expire and must be renewed, and each member can only issue a limited number of them;
- the network checks that every member is close enough to the rest of the web.

The blockchain is secured by members' computers running the free software **Duniter**. Blocks are written in turn by members, with a very light proof of work adjusted to prevent any single node from dominating. A Raspberry Pi is enough to run a node: the energy footprint is negligible compared to Bitcoin.

## Using Ğ1 in practice

- **Wallets.** The most popular clients are [Cesium](https://cesium.app/) (web and mobile) and its successors, all free software.
- **Marketplaces.** Members exchange goods and services on [ğchange](https://www.gchange.fr/) and at local markets ("ğmarchés"), mostly in France, Belgium and Spain.
- **Community.** Local groups organise meetups to meet each other and exchange certifications.

You can use Ğ1 without becoming a member (you just won't co-create the dividend), but the real experience comes from joining the Web of Trust.

## Limits and open questions

Ğ1 is a fascinating experiment, but let's stay lucid:

- the economy is still small and mostly based on second-hand goods and local services;
- the Web of Trust requires meeting people physically, which is slow (and that is intended);
- the currency has no legal tender status and should not be seen as an investment.

## Why it matters

Ğ1 shows that a blockchain does not have to be an ecological disaster, and that money creation is a **political and technical design choice**, not a law of nature. It is also a beautiful example of a **free software** project, entirely developed by volunteers, licensed under open licences and governed by its community.
